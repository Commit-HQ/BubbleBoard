# Architecture

One SvelteKit application, deployed as one Cloudflare Worker with static assets. Keep client code in `src/lib`, server-only helpers in `src/lib/server`, and HTTP endpoints in `src/routes`. Use strict TypeScript and Svelte 5 runes. Module-level constants such as lookup tables and asset lists are fine; never keep per-user or per-request mutable state at module level on the server.

## Boundaries

- Browser: card reading, key derivation, encryption and decryption, and the photo processing of events, in the [access format](access-format.md). `src/lib/kindergarten.ts` opens and builds the records, and `src/lib/app/state.svelte.ts` holds what a device may see and the changes it makes.
- Worker: sessions, authorization, request checks, and storing ciphertext (`src/lib/server`, `src/routes/api`); and notifications carrying only which kind of thing happened, sent through a queue (`src/lib/server/push.ts`), whose handler, with the daily cleanup (`src/lib/server/cleanup.ts`), `worker/index.js` adds to SvelteKit's fetch handler.
- D1: opaque identifiers, hashes of card and session tokens, and encrypted records, with the schema in `migrations/`. `npm run deploy` publishes before migrating, because the first deploy creates the database, so keep each migration compatible with the code already running.
- Private R2: encrypted photos and files, fetched through authorized Worker routes: the photo of each classroom's board (`src/lib/server/photos.ts`), the files on notices (`src/lib/server/notices.ts`), the files on info pages (`src/lib/server/info.ts`), the photos of events (`src/lib/server/events.ts`), and the files a teacher attaches to an inquiry (`src/lib/server/messages.ts`). Every read and write goes through `src/lib/server/storage.ts`, which counts each object's bytes and each month's uploads and downloads in D1 before R2 is touched, and refuses more past the `STORAGE_*` limits in `.env` (`src/lib/server/limits.ts`), so an installation stays within R2's free allowance. Records name the objects they keep (the `named_objects` view in `migrations/`, which each new kind of record that keeps bytes joins, with a trigger that marks its object as on its way out when the record goes, however it goes); a change deletes the objects it marked once it's saved, and the daily cleanup deletes those a change didn't finish, and uploads no record named a day later. A key is stored only once, and an object is marked as on its way out before its bytes are deleted, so no upload replaces bytes a record names and no record names bytes being deleted. Requests that change something run to their end even if the device disconnects midway (`src/hooks.server.ts`).

The app sets up a kindergarten, manages classrooms, teachers, children, and family cards, lets a family's devices add its other devices with one-time cards, and puts notices, with their polls and files, and a photo of each classroom's corkboard on the board. The head of the kindergarten writes its info pages, with their files, which everyone sees, and a head or the classroom's lead records from a child's consent form whether the classroom's families may see that child's face. Teachers publish events, whose photos cover every face a family may not see, and attach files to the private inquiries they answer. Those photos and files are the only uploads: staff send them, encrypted in the browser, through authorized endpoints. The access boundary was reviewed and tried on real phones before real classroom information was accepted (2026-09-13); review it again for every new endpoint, and do not add public upload endpoints. Records are encrypted from the first one persisted; there is no plaintext version to migrate later.

## Pages and languages

Pages live in two route groups. `(marketing)` holds the landing pages, prerendered to static HTML without client-side JavaScript (`prerender` and `csr` in its `+layout.ts`), except the walk through the app below. `(app)` holds the app: pages prerendered as static shells that then run in the browser. Their HTML carries no classroom data, and the server never renders any; protected data arrives as ciphertext and is decrypted on the device. The root layout only adds the stylesheet, favicon, and locale.

App pages are prerendered once, so a record's ID travels in the query (`/app/child?id=…`) and pages read it in the browser; IDs aren't secret. The header of a connected device's app pages links to the kindergarten's info page and to Settings, which hold the language, and staff also get Manage, with the classrooms and teachers; until a device connects, the header has the language switch instead, as on the landing page. Every app page ends with a footer linking to the landing page and showing the build's commit; the privacy policy is linked from the landing pages' footer. Prerendering starts from the default language's pages and finds the other language's through each app page's `hreflang` alternate links. The `(app)` layout creates one `App` state for every app page through context, and starts it after the first navigation, once the router can take a card or setup token out of the address bar; a link opened while the app shows changes only the fragment, which the layout also watches. App pages keep the title "BubbleBoard", so tab titles and browser history never hold a name.

On phones and tablets, the app works only once installed (`src/lib/install.ts`). iPhone and iPad connect nothing outside the Home Screen app, whose storage Safari doesn't share, and show how to add it; Safari keeps a card link's fragment in the address bar there, and those browsers get a manifest without `start_url`, so the Home Screen app opens at the card link it was added from and connects with that card, once. Android connects in the browser, whose storage the installed app shares, then asks to be installed; browsers inside other apps ask to open the page in a real browser. Computers use the app in the browser. Each language has a web app manifest (`/app/manifest.webmanifest`, `/en/app/manifest.webmanifest`) with the same app ID and scope, which the Worker renders because it depends on the browser asking, and its icons in `static/icons` are renderings of `src/lib/assets/app-icon.svg`.

The service worker (`src/service-worker.ts`) handles notifications only and caches nothing. A push carries one letter naming one of five kinds — a notice or board photo, a message, meeting times to book, a booking that changed, or an event's new photos (`src/lib/push.ts`) — and the service worker holds the words for each, as the notification's title, in the language notifications were turned on in, which `src/lib/notifications.ts` keeps in IndexedDB for it; devices already say which app a notification is from. The kind is also the notification's tag and the push's `Topic`, so a message replaces an earlier message without burying a cancelled meeting time, and it chooses the page a tap opens. A push also tells the app's open windows to load the board again, and a tap brings a window forward, telling it the same, or opens the app. Turning notifications on asks for permission straight from the tap, as Safari requires, then subscribes with the installation's public VAPID key and sends the keys the browser made for that subscription. The app sends its subscription whenever it opens, which keeps it with the current session, and loads the board again when it comes back into view. A subscription made with an earlier key gets no pushes, so the app renews it with the installation's current key first; a browser that won't subscribe without a tap ends it instead, and notifications show as off until they're turned on again. Brave refuses to subscribe until its privacy settings allow Google's push service, which Chromium browsers push through, so when subscribing fails there the app names that setting.

Croatian is served at `/` and `/app`, English at `/en` and `/en/app`; the optional `[[locale=locale]]` route segment and `src/params/locale.ts` keep the default language unprefixed. `src/lib/paths.ts` builds every path, including the language switch's link to the same page in the other language, and `src/lib/card.ts` writes and reads card codes and their links. Card links are read by their origin and fragment only, so printed cards keep working in either language; `/app` and `/en/app` must always open the app. The language switch, in the landing page's header and in the app's Settings, or its header until a device connects, is a plain link, so no cookie is needed. `src/hooks.server.ts` and the root layout take the language from the path (`pathLocale`), so an address no page has gets the root error page, a popped bubble, in the language the address asks for.

The landing page is identical on every installation: it names no kindergarten, and its contact details belong to the project (`src/lib/project.ts`). It describes the finished product in the present tense, while the [decisions](decisions.md) record why it behaves as it does. Its main action opens the app, where families connect with their card, and a second link leads kindergartens without BubbleBoard to the contact section.

The walk through the app (`/explore/parents`, `/explore/teachers`, and the same under `/en`) is a landing page too, and the only one that runs in the browser (`csr` in its `+page.ts`). Its steps sit side by side in a row that snaps as it's swiped, and each step carries its own links to the steps before and after it and its own dots, so the page works as plain HTML, where following a link jumps to its step. In the browser the same links slide the row without moving the page, focus follows to the new step's control, and the arrow keys do the same. The landing pages' layout makes every link load a document (`data-sveltekit-reload`), so this page never pulls the others in as scripts. Its copy is `explore` in both dictionaries, one entry a step, with what each screen shows for screen readers. The screens (`src/lib/assets/explore`, drawn in a phone by `src/lib/components/Screenshot.svelte`) are real ones, taken in each language from a local installation that was set up with made-up families, notices, and conversations and the landing page's stock photos, never from an installation with real families. Take them again when a screen they show changes: 390 by 844 points at three times the density, then as the photos are, at 390 and 780 px wide.

The privacy policy (`/privacy`, `/en/privacy`) is a landing page too: a few short points on what an installation stores, where, and for how long, with the project's contact address. It describes what the code does (`privacyPolicy` in both dictionaries), so update it in the same change as anything that alters those. The session cookie is the only cookie and is strictly necessary, so there is no consent banner; keep it that way.

Canonical URLs, `hreflang` alternates, and link-preview tags need an absolute address, which prerendered pages can't take from a request. It comes from the required build-time `PUBLIC_SITE_URL` in `.env`, read with `$env/static/public`; Worker `vars` don't exist at build time and can't change generated HTML. `src/lib/project.ts` accepts only an `https://` origin (or `http://` for localhost) without credentials, path, query, or fragment, and otherwise stops the build with a message. Build absolute links with its `absoluteUrl`. The storage limits are build-time settings in `.env` too, read with `$env/static/private` one variable at a time, so the rest of the build machine's environment stays out of the bundle; modules the daily cleanup imports can't read them, because Wrangler bundles those without SvelteKit.

All interface copy lives in `src/lib/i18n/en.ts` and `hr.ts`. Croatian must satisfy the same TypeScript shape as English; add each new key to both dictionaries. Keep sentences whole rather than assembling translated fragments: copy that names something is a function in each dictionary, so each language builds its own sentence, plural, and list (`Intl.PluralRules` and `Intl.ListFormat`). Keep links out of translated strings: put the linked words in markup, as the footer does for Commit. User-authored classroom content is not automatically translated. The interface calls a card a QR code ("QR kod"), which parents understand; code and docs keep the word card.

## API

JSON endpoints live in `src/routes/api`, one small handler per user action. A handler first checks who is asking (`requireIdentity`, `requireStaff`, `requireManager`, or `requireHead`, or `limitAttempts` for setup and cards, in `src/lib/server/session.ts`), reads its body with the readers in `validate.ts`, and makes the change through `catalog.ts`, where each change is one D1 batch and so one transaction. A head's change takes the `Head` that `requireHead` returns, and a change to children or family cards the `Manager` that `requireManager` returns, which carries the role the server then scopes a lead by, so a change can't skip the check, and responds with the records as they are now; a staff session comes with its records too, so the app never asks for them separately. Record IDs in paths must match `src/params/id.ts`. The server can't read profiles, so it enforces structure: who may ask, the form of every field, foreign keys, a trigger that keeps a head, and a catalog revision. A change to a teacher, children, or family cards names the revision its device read, and the database refuses it when another change came first, so two heads can't undo each other's work, and a form left open can't restore rights another head withdrew. Errors are `error(status, code)`, and the app explains each code in both dictionaries (`app.errors`).

## Dependency policy

The event photo editor uses pinned MediaPipe Tasks Vision 0.10.32 in a classic Web Worker, with a locally served BlazeFace short-range model. `scripts/face-detector-assets.js` copies the package's CommonJS runtime unchanged to a versioned `.js` asset during install/dev/build: Cloudflare's `.cjs` MIME type is not executable in browsers. Vite emits both WASM variants and their loaders; only the supported variant is loaded at runtime. No CDN or remote inference is used. Detection can fail or miss faces; the manual workflow and review are always available. `src/lib/events` holds the editor's geometry, detection and covered-raster preparation; `package.ts` prepares and renders encrypted event photos, while `server/events.ts` controls consent, staging, publication and retrieval. See [event format](events-format.md). The detector scans the full image and overlapping half-size tiles in four orientations; no additional model download is needed.

Use platform APIs and Svelte first. Add a library only for an implemented need, after checking maintenance, licensing, bundle cost, and transitive dependencies. Tailwind CSS is the styling standard. No component UI library, ORM, global state package, date library, analytics, remote fonts, or image editor is needed for this foundation.

`qr` (MIT, no dependencies) encodes cards' QR codes, which `src/lib/app/qr.ts` draws as a bubble full of SVG dots with the app icon, and reads them from the camera (`qr/dom.js`) or a photo. Its decoder loads only when someone scans a card, and `qr.test.ts` reads the drawing back with it. Tiptap 3 (MIT, on ProseMirror) is the editor of notices and info pages, imported only when a form's editor mounts. Its injected stylesheet is off for the CSP, with its rules in `app.css`, and boards don't use it: they render a notice's checked JSON document with Svelte components (`NoticeBody.svelte`). `libheif-js` (LGPL-3.0, no dependencies), libheif compiled to WebAssembly, opens HEIC and HEIF photos, which phones such as Samsung's save and only Safari opens itself (`src/lib/heif.ts`). It's about 700 KB compressed, so it loads only when a picture the browser couldn't open is one, and the CSP allows compiling WebAssembly for it. Load future photo tooling only in the routes that use it.

Vitest is the one test runner, a development dependency only. It reuses the Vite and SvelteKit configuration, so tests import `$lib` modules as the app does, and it runs the same Web Crypto API in Node. Test behavior the code guarantees, such as encryption, card links, and authorization, not component markup. Database tests run the real migrations in Node's built-in SQLite behind a shared D1-shaped adapter (`src/lib/server/test-database.ts`), with R2 in memory (`src/lib/server/catalog.test.ts`), which keeps them fast and needs no Workers runtime. `fake-indexeddb` (Apache-2.0, no dependencies), also for development only, gives the tests of what a device keeps in IndexedDB a real store to run against in Node.

## Design

The look is cheerful glassmorphism: translucent white surfaces over soft coral, violet, sky, and sun gradients, with ink pill actions. Style with Tailwind CSS utilities in markup; Prettier sorts the classes. `src/lib/styles/app.css`, the only stylesheet, holds the theme tokens (`ink`, `muted`, `canvas`, `accent`, `blush`, `apricot`, `font-sans`, `font-display`), the utilities below, and base styles. Prefer theme tokens and Tailwind's default scale over arbitrary values, and avoid component `<style>` blocks unless Tailwind cannot express the rule.

- `glass` is the surface for cards and sections. It has no backdrop blur, which over the soft page backdrop would look nearly the same and cost painting while scrolling. It creates no stacking context, so add `isolate` when a child uses a negative z-index.
- `frosted` adds the blur, with a nearly opaque fallback where `backdrop-filter` is unsupported. Use it only where content moves or sits behind the surface: the sticky header and overlays on photos.
- `bg-sunrise` is the decorative brand gradient for icon tiles. `text-sunrise` is its version for heading text, with darker stops, and falls back to plain text in forced-colours mode.

App screens reuse these surfaces: `glass` panels and tiles, the buttons, fields, and checkboxes drawn as cards in `src/lib/app/ui.ts`, `bg-sunrise` icon tiles, and a few static soap bubbles. Printed pages drop the backdrop and surfaces (`app.css` and `print:` variants): card sheets come out on white, two cards to a row with a dashed edge to cut along, so a classroom's cards take little ink. Questions before a change that can't be undone use the native `<dialog>` (`ConfirmDialog.svelte`), as does a board photo or a notice's picture on the whole screen (`PictureViewer.svelte`); forms read `FormData` when submitted.

Palette colours are not automatically text colours: blush and apricot are too light for text on this page. Check text contrast (4.5:1, or 3:1 for large headings) against the tinted backdrop, not only the canvas. Don't let colour alone mark a state; forced colours remove fills.

Fonts are final: Hedvig Letters Serif for headings and Hanken Grotesk for text, self-hosted from Fontsource files (OFL) and never loaded from a font CDN. `app.css` declares only the Latin and Latin Extended subsets, with metric-matched local fallbacks (Arial and Georgia, values from Capsize) so text doesn't shift when the fonts swap in. `src/hooks.server.ts` preloads the Latin files on every page and the Latin Extended files on Croatian pages; app pages, which open with body text, skip the display font.

Photos are real stock images committed pre-optimized as AVIF/WebP, with sources and processing notes in `src/lib/assets/photos/CREDITS.md`. `src/lib/components/Photo.svelte` lists them by name, so a wrong name fails type checking and a missing file fails the build. Give each placement a `sizes` value that matches its layout, and set `eager` only on the photo in the first view. Only the landing page uses stock photos; the app shows only the photos teachers take of their boards and the pictures they attach to notices, decrypted in the browser. Icons come from `src/lib/components/Icon.svelte`. Photo, font, icon, and library licenses are in `static/third-party-notices.txt`, which every installation serves; update it when bundling a new asset or library. No remote images, remote fonts, or animation library.

Use semantic HTML, visible focus, comfortable touch targets, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion support. Only real links should look and behave like links. A link that leaves the app, such as About BubbleBoard, a build's commit, or a link in a notice, opens in a window of its own (`target="_blank"`), because the installed app has no way back. Long words break anywhere (`overflow-wrap: anywhere` in `app.css`), so user content such as a file's name or a link never widens a page; don't set `nowrap` on it. Extract components when they represent a reusable concept, not simply to split a file. Keep parent flows short and use defaults instead of repeated configuration choices. App pages show a real loading state while they start, and errors say what to do next in both languages.

## Security direction

The target is protection of stored content without server-held decryption keys. An operator who can modify delivered JavaScript remains trusted. Client compromise, shared QR cards, and saved photos remain outside that protection. Revocation blocks server access; it cannot erase retained keys or downloaded content. Every staff card opens the same Staff Key, so teachers are kept to their classrooms by authorization, not encryption ([access format](access-format.md)); check every new endpoint and query against that.

A hash-mode CSP (`csp` in `vite.config.ts`) allows same-origin resources only, and images from the `blob:` URLs the app makes for photos it decrypted, no inline scripts or styles beyond SvelteKit's hashed ones, no plugins, and no framing. Prerendered pages carry it as a `<meta>` tag and get the other security headers, including `frame-ancestors`, from `_headers`; Worker-rendered responses get them from `src/hooks.server.ts`. Keep the two header lists in sync and keep them strict: self-host new assets rather than widening directives. App pages run SvelteKit in the browser, whose route announcer has one fixed inline style attribute: `style-src-attr` allows exactly that value by hash (`'unsafe-hashes'`, never `'unsafe-inline'`), and `src/csp.test.ts` fails when a SvelteKit update changes it. Style with classes, not inline style attributes. Documents on notices and info pages are saved as downloads, never opened in the app, with a type from a fixed list chosen by their name (`src/lib/files.ts`), so no file opens as a page with the app's origin. Pictures on notices and info pages show, as board photos do, only as JPEG, PNG, or WebP images checked by their bytes, from `blob:` URLs, and WebP is saved as PNG.

A device connects once with a card and then uses a session: a random 256-bit token in an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie limited to `/api`, stored only as its SHA-256 hash, and ending after 90 days without use. Replacing a card, or removing the teacher or family it belongs to, deletes its sessions, and replacing a family's card also ends its one-time cards with the sessions they started. Requests that change something under `/api` must carry this site's `Origin` (`src/hooks.server.ts`), bodies are JSON of at most 64 KB, apart from the encrypted bytes of a board photo of at most 3 MB, with its encrypted details in a header, or of a notice's file of at most 10 MB, uploads and downloads stay within the storage limits, and setup and card attempts are rate limited per address (`CARD_ATTEMPTS` in `wrangler.jsonc`). The setup token, a Worker secret, allows only the first setup, and a setup link carries it in the fragment.

Card secrets stay in URL fragments: never put them in paths, queries, request bodies, storage, or logs. The one exception is outside the app's control: on iPhone and iPad, a card link's fragment stays in Safari's address bar so the Home Screen app can open with it, and iOS keeps that address in Safari's history and with the Home Screen icon ([access format](access-format.md)). A family device's one-time card is meant to be sent, in a chat for example, which is why it connects only one device, within a day. Keys are created and used with Web Crypto as non-extractable `CryptoKey` objects; that prevents accidental export, not misuse by a script running in the app, so XSS remains critical (product-spec.md §36).

The footers of the landing page and every app page show the commit a build came from (product specification §47), marked as modified when the build had uncommitted changes, including new files Git doesn't ignore, and an unknown build when there was no Git metadata (for example, a source ZIP). A commit is provenance, not proof that a deployed tree matches it. Publishing a build manifest and verifying deployed output belong with automated deploys. Before real data (2026-09-13), every endpoint's access was reviewed, requests without a session were refused on the live installation, a database export and the stored objects were checked for anything readable, cards and browser storage were tried on real iPhone and Android devices, and backup and restore were documented ([hosting](hosting.md#backup-and-restore)). Review the access boundary and the CSP again for each new feature; retention, deletion, and notification enrollment come with their features. Never log QR secrets, tokens, plaintext content, or request bodies. Keep push text generic: a push carries only which of five kinds of thing happened, never a name, a word of content, or a record's ID, so new photos say only that there are new photos and never which event or whose child, and that letter is encrypted to the one device it's for (RFC 8291), so a push service reads nothing and every push is the same size. Pushes go only to the push services browsers use, and are signed with the installation's `VAPID_KEY` secret, which is never replaced, because devices subscribe with its public key. Cloudflare never shows a secret again, so `scripts/push-key.js` shows a new key once, to be kept in a password manager. No third-party runtime scripts or user-controlled HTML.

The [product specification](product-spec.md) holds the requirements. It is the original target, not a statement of implemented guarantees, and its opening note lists the decisions that have since replaced parts of it. Follow that note where they differ, and reconcile the rest with it before implementing a feature.

## Dependency audit

On 2026-09-11, `npm audit` reported one low-severity advisory in SvelteKit’s transitive `cookie` dependency ([GHSA-pxg6-pf52-xh8x](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-pxg6-pf52-xh8x)), propagated to three package entries, and it still did on 2026-09-12. The suggested automatic fix downgrades SvelteKit incompatibly, so it was not applied. The advisory concerns cookie names, paths, and domains taken from untrusted input; the session cookie's name, path, and attributes are fixed in `src/lib/server/session.ts`. Keep them fixed, and recheck upstream when updating SvelteKit.

## Family inquiries

`src/lib/messages.ts` holds shared policy types, Zagreb calendar calculations and browser encryption. `src/lib/server/messages.ts` owns the D1 conversation boundary; routes in `api/messages` authenticate, validate encrypted envelopes and dispatch notifications. A teacher's message can carry files: they reuse `src/lib/files.ts` and the notice file envelope, their bytes go up before the message that names them, and `message_files` joins `named_objects` so they are counted, deleted with their inquiry, and swept by the daily cleanup like every other upload. Families attach nothing, which the server enforces. Per-classroom policies are saved by a head or the classroom's lead, with revision checks. The insertion SQL repeats membership, sending-window, closed-state and monthly quota checks at write time. A family message is charged by the same statement that inserts it: `messages.charged` holds the Zagreb month it spent, or NULL when a teacher wrote the conversation's last message, so an answer that arrives mid-request can never be charged for, and the quota is the count of charged messages that family has in that classroom and month. Creating the conversation and its first message is one batch transaction. Stable client-generated message and conversation IDs make identical retries idempotent; reusing an ID with different content is refused. Closing a conversation never releases quota.

The inbox loads encrypted titles and only the latest message per conversation, which clients decrypt for search and previews. Every classroom's policy comes from two grouped queries rather than a pair per classroom. Individual conversations fetch 50 messages at a time by sequence, and read progress advances monotonically per family or teacher. The app refreshes the inbox alongside the rest of its state, without holding the board up for it, and on visible message pages every 30 seconds until nobody has touched the page for five minutes. A staff device opens each family's key once per envelope and keeps it, so a classroom's conversations cost one unwrap, not one each. The inbox itself is not paginated: it returns every conversation of every visible classroom, which for a head is the whole kindergarten. Conversations go when a family leaves a classroom, so this stays small for a year's use; page it server-side before it doesn't. Private-message queue entries carry a conversation ID; delivery rechecks active sessions and current membership before contacting endpoints, including on retries. Deleting a family/classroom membership cascades to its conversations, messages and read state.

Inbox refresh reuses decrypted subjects and previews when their ciphertext, record context and key are unchanged; read and closed state always come from the new response. The cache belongs to the connected app, retains only the current inbox and clears on disconnect. A conversation refresh keeps loaded history only when it overlaps the latest page; otherwise it restarts pagination there so older messages remain reachable.

## Individual meetings

The home card leads both roles to `/app/meetings`. Staff generate time slots from a date, window and duration, exclude breaks, then publish. Families select a child when needed and reserve or cancel a time; staff see child names and may cancel their own offers' bookings (a head, and a lead in her own classrooms, may manage every offer there). The same page groups upcoming times by day and offer and keeps past times collapsed. Native date/time inputs and existing controls keep this flow usable without a calendar dependency.

`src/lib/meetings.ts` handles Zagreb times and shared types; `src/lib/server/meetings.ts` enforces access and atomic writes. See the individual meetings section of `access-format.md` for metadata, encryption and invitation lifecycle. Migration `0016_meetings.sql` must be applied before serving the updated app.

The home notification opt-in precedes the meetings card. Families see the card only while their classrooms have upcoming slots; staff retain it to publish new ones. Staff can remove all upcoming slots they manage for one classroom and Zagreb calendar day, across offers, with an explicit reservation count and confirmation. A single conditional delete checks the complete list and each version before removing anything; bookings changed since confirmation require a fresh review. Booked families receive one notification per device, saying a booking changed and nothing more.
