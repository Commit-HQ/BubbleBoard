# Decisions

Why BubbleBoard behaves the way it does. Each entry is a decision that the code alone doesn't explain: a trade-off that was weighed, a simpler option that won, or a rule that must not be quietly undone. Everything here describes the app as it is; what was tried and dropped appears only where the reason still matters. Git history holds the order things happened in.

Most of these came from the owner, from teachers, and from parents using the first installation. Event photos have two pages of their own: the [editor](events-editor.md) and the [format](events-format.md). The keys behind all of it are in the [access format](access-format.md).

## The board

BubbleBoard replaces the corkboard in the kindergarten hallway, so putting up a notice should be as easy and cheerful as pinning a note to it.

- **Home is the board.** Everyone's home shows notices, newest first, each on its paper colour with its classrooms, its author, and when it went up, as "Ana Horvat · 23.01.2026 u 8:30" ("23/01/2026 at 8:30" in English). Admins see every classroom's notices, teachers their classrooms', and families their children's classrooms'; a family with children in two of a notice's classrooms sees it once. A family that joins later sees the notices that are still up, as on the real board. With more than one classroom, a filter shows one classroom's notices and starts on all of them; a single classroom isn't named, at a parent's request.
- **Staff on home:** Add notice, with a paper icon, and Post the board, with a board icon. Home greets staff by name, by the device's clock: Croatian uses morning until 10, daytime until 18, and evening after; English says good morning until noon, good afternoon until 18, and good evening after. Families and the recovery card aren't greeted — a family device can't read the name on its QR code, which is encrypted for staff, the recovery card has no name, and a parent found a greeting without a name unnecessary.
- **Seen marks:** families mark a notice as seen with a tap, so teachers know which families saw it. A mark is something a family does; nothing records who opened what. An unmarked notice has an accent outline and a Mark as seen button, and a marked one says Seen. Under each notice, staff see how many of its families marked it and, when they open that line, which have and which haven't, among the families of the classrooms they see. Families don't see each other's marks. A change that notifies everyone again clears the marks, so everyone is asked to see it again. The server stores which family marked which notice: opaque IDs, but activity it didn't hold before, which the owner chose (spec §44).
- **Polls:** a notice can carry a poll. Add a poll, under the notice's text in its form, takes two to ten short answers and says to write the question in the notice's text. A family ticks an answer and confirms it with Confirm answer; the answer then takes the choices' place, as "Your answer: …", with Change answer to bring them back, so it's clear whether the poll was answered. Answering marks the notice as seen. Staff see, under each answer, how many of the notice's families chose it, a bar, their names, and then the families that haven't answered. An answer keeps its votes when its words are edited, and taking the poll off removes them. Showing results to families, choosing several answers, and closing a poll are left for later.
- **Poll counts:** a teacher decides, with the poll, whether families see how many families chose each answer. Such a poll holds a Poll Key inside its notice's envelope, and families encrypt their answers with it instead of their Family Key, so every family that opens the notice counts them, beside each answer with a bar, never seeing who. The server knows which polls show counts and sends their answers, with families' random IDs, to the notice's families. Changing the setting on a poll that has answers removes them, because they were encrypted for the other way, and the form says so before saving: encrypting each answer twice would keep them, but the simplest safe option won.
- **Board photo:** teachers used to photograph the hallway corkboard and send it to the parents' chat group, and the app keeps that habit. Post the board puts a photo of a classroom's board up for its families, captioned Photo of the notice board with the classroom, who put it up, and when. It takes the place of the classroom's earlier photo and stays until a new one replaces it or a teacher of the classroom or an admin takes it down, rather than for a number of days (spec §42), and it doesn't go through the photo consent workflow, because it shows the board with no children in it; the owner decided both. Families see it at the top of home, following the board's classroom filter, and a tap opens it on the whole screen, where a tap zooms in to read what's pinned up. Putting one up notifies the classroom as a new notice does.
- **Pull to refresh:** pulling home down from the top loads the board again, which the installed app had no other way to do, with an arrow under the header that spins while it loads. While home is open, the browser's own pull to reload and the page's bounce are off. A load that fails, such as while a phone's connection wakes up, doesn't keep the next return to the app from loading the board.

## Notices

- **Audience:** a notice goes to the classrooms ticked when posting: a teacher's own, or any or all for an admin. Where there is only one, it is ticked and can't be unticked.
- **Writing:** a rich editor with bold, lists, links, and the notice's paper colour, one of about six soft colours from the app's palette, or white. The author's name is stored inside the encrypted notice, because families can't open teacher records; a notice posted with the recovery card has no author name.
- **Keeping and changing:** the teacher chooses how long the notice stays — 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, or 90 days, with 30 preselected — counted from when it was first posted. The author and admins can change everything about a notice, its classrooms and days included, and choose whether the change notifies everyone again; a changed notice says it was edited, and one that notifies again moves to the top. They can also delete it at once. A removed teacher's notices stay until they expire or an admin deletes them. Expired and deleted notices leave every board at its next load, and the server deletes them (spec §52).
- **Encryption:** every save makes a new random Notice Key. The notice's text, colour, and author name are encrypted once with it, and the key is wrapped with the Group Key of each chosen classroom: a notice for twenty classrooms is one envelope and twenty small keys, and a classroom taken off a notice can't open its later versions. Staff open Group Keys with the Staff Key, families with their Family Key. The server stores the notice's ID, the author's teacher ID, its classrooms and times, and the envelopes.

### The editor

Tiptap 3 (MIT), chosen by the owner. It doesn't depend on a framework, so the notice form creates and destroys the editor itself, without a wrapper package, and the editor loads only on the page where notices are written.

- **Tools:** bold, bullet and numbered lists, links, and background colour — the notice's paper, offered in the toolbar so the notice is written on the colour it will have. Markdown typed as you go, such as `- ` for a list or `**bold**`, formats the text. StarterKit's headings, code, quotes, strikethrough, underline, and rules are off, so a notice stays simple.
- **What was taken out, and why it stays out:** text colours, because Tiptap's Color extension writes inline `style` attributes that the CSP blocks, and carrying them as a mark of our own wasn't worth it; italic, dropped after a round of feedback; and the emoji picker, because phone keyboards have emoji and bundling an emoji data set for the few devices without one wasn't worth its weight. Text that had any of them renders plain, and saving it again drops them.
- **Storage:** a notice keeps Tiptap's JSON document inside its encrypted envelope. Boards check it against the allowed nodes and marks and render it with the app's own components, never as HTML (spec §36). Links open only `https:` and `mailto:` addresses, and outside the app's window, so the installed app keeps its place.
- **CSP:** the editor's injected stylesheet is off (`injectCSS: false`), with its few rules in `app.css`, and the paper colour is a class from a fixed set, because the CSP allows no inline styles.

## Files and pictures

- **Files on notices:** a notice carries up to ten files, attached in its form under the poll: PDFs and Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, and text documents of up to 10 MB each, and pictures. Everyone who sees the notice sees its files under its text, with their names and sizes. A file's type comes from its name, from that fixed list, never from its bytes, so a file never opens as a page with the app's origin. Each file is encrypted with a File Key of its own, which travels inside the notice's envelope with the file's name, so saving the notice again under a new Notice Key doesn't upload its files again. The device seals each file as it's attached and uploads it just before it saves the notice, which then names it, so trying again after a failure uploads the same bytes. A device that doesn't know about files, such as an app not updated yet, is refused when it saves a notice rather than silently taking the notice's files off. The server gives a file's bytes only to devices that see the notice, deletes the files a change leaves out and those of a deleted notice at once, and deletes the files of expired notices, or of a notice that was never saved, in the daily cleanup. It knows which notice carries which files and how big they are, not what they're called.
- **Pictures** show under the notice as small squares, and a tap opens one on the whole screen, as a board photo opens, where a tap zooms in. Documents keep their rows, which save them. A notice's pictures are fetched once it comes near the screen, so a long board doesn't fetch pictures no one scrolls to, and they open only when their bytes are a JPEG, PNG, or WebP image.
- **Preparing a photo:** the browser makes it at most 2560 pixels on its longer side and encodes it again, which also leaves out the camera's metadata, such as location, before it is encrypted. Where the browser can't write WebP, as Safari on iPhone can't, a picture with see-through pixels is attached as PNG rather than JPEG, which would fill them with black.
- **Saving pictures:** the whole-screen view has Save. A device saves WebP as PNG, which not every iPhone opens otherwise, and JPEG and PNG as they are; the owner chose PNG, which keeps see-through pixels, over JPEG, which would take a fraction of the space. On iPhone and iPad, Save opens the share sheet, whose Save Image puts the picture in Photos, where people look for it; elsewhere it's a download.
- **HEIC:** a teacher's phone saves photos as HEIC, which only Safari opens. Where the browser can't, libheif compiled to WebAssembly (`libheif-js`, LGPL-3.0) opens it in the browser and the photo is prepared like any other, so the server still gets it only encrypted. Board photos, pictures on notices, and a QR code read from a photo all open this way. libheif is about 700 KB compressed and loads only for such a photo, which takes a moment longer on a phone. The CSP allows compiling WebAssembly (`'wasm-unsafe-eval'`) for it.
- **Long names:** a file's name wraps onto at most two lines, and a notice's text breaks long links and words anywhere, so neither widens the page.

## Notifications and installing

- **Who is notified:** devices that turned notifications on, for a family in one of the notice's classrooms or a teacher assigned to one, except the device that posted. Admins aren't notified for classrooms they aren't assigned to.
- **What a notification says:** the words for one of five kinds of thing — "New notice from your kindergarten", "New message", "New meeting times", "Meeting time changed", and new event photos — localised into the language notifications were turned on in, and nothing else: no name, no word of content, no record's ID. The words are its title, with no body, because phones already say which app it's from and a title of "BubbleBoard" read as "BubbleBoard from BubbleBoard".
- **Turning them on:** a connected device's home shows a card, "Get a notification when there's a new notice", with Turn on and Not now. Turn on asks for permission from that tap, then sends a test notification saying only that notifications are on. Not now hides the card on that device; Settings always have the switch. If permission was refused, the card explains how to allow it in the device's settings, because the browser won't ask again. Where push can't work, the card isn't shown.
- **Delivery:** a push carries one letter naming which kind of thing happened and nothing else, encrypted to the one device it's for (RFC 8291), because a browser accepts no payload otherwise; the words for each kind live in the service worker. Every letter is one byte, so every push is the same size. Every push shows a notification, as Safari requires. A push, and a tap on its notification, tells the app's open windows to load the board straight away, so a notice that arrives while the app is open is there when the notification is tapped. A device that subscribed before its keys were stored gets an empty push, which reads as a notice, until its app next opens and sends them. Publishing puts the devices to notify on a Cloudflare Queue in groups of 40, below the Free plan's 50 outgoing requests per invocation; the same Worker sends each group with the installation's VAPID key, retries 429 and 5xx responses later, and deletes subscriptions the push service reports gone (404 and 410). SvelteKit's Cloudflare adapter builds only a `fetch` handler, so a small Worker entry file adds the queue consumer and the daily cleanup.
- **Subscriptions** belong to the session that created them (spec §39): signing out, a replaced card, a removed teacher or family, or an expired session deletes them. The app sends its subscription whenever it opens, which keeps it current and moves it to a new session. The VAPID key pair is one Worker secret per installation, created when missing, and never rotated: a new key silently ends every subscription.
- **Installing on phones and tablets is required.** Notifications are why parents use BubbleBoard, and on iPhone and iPad they work only in the Home Screen app, so on phones and tablets BubbleBoard works only once installed. That holds for every card, because a card link doesn't say whose card it is. On Android, the browser connects first, because Chrome's installed app shares its connection, then asks to install, so the parent scans only once. An in-app browser that can't install, such as a chat app's, asks to open the link in Safari or Chrome. Computers work in the browser, where installing is optional.
- **One scan on iPhone and iPad:** the Home Screen app doesn't share Safari's storage, so the only thing that reaches it is the address it opens at. Safari keeps a card link's fragment in the address bar, and browsers there get a manifest without `start_url`, for which WebKit takes the page's address, fragment included, so BubbleBoard added from a card link opens with that card and connects. It uses that card only the first time, since the Home Screen icon keeps the address: signing out, or a card replaced since, stays that way. The trade-off, accepted for the simpler install, is that the code stays in Safari's history and in the icon's address. Where iOS drops the fragment, the app asks for the QR code as before.
- **Manifest:** one per language, starting at `/app` or `/en/app`, with the same app ID and scope, PNG icons, and an Apple touch icon. The service worker handles only pushes and notification taps: no caching, no offline copies. Chrome shows its own install prompt only to sites whose service worker handles requests, so Android usually shows the menu steps, which need no service worker.

## Info pages

Teachers asked for pages with what everyone at the kindergarten should know, such as opening hours, contacts, and meals, which admins write.

- **Info** sits in the header of every connected device, just before Settings. On phones it shows only its icon, as Manage does, and on the narrowest phones a staff header leaves out the name beside the logo so all three fit. Everyone sees the same pages, whatever their classrooms: families of every classroom, teachers, those without classrooms included, and admins. Until an admin adds one, Info says there's nothing there yet.
- **Pages:** each is a paper of its own, as a notice is, with its text on a paper colour and up to ten files of the kinds and sizes a notice carries, and says when it was last updated. They show one under another, without titles; a page can start with bold text, such as "Meals". A kindergarten keeps up to 20. Pages have no days, classrooms, polls, or seen marks, stay until an admin changes or deletes them, and saving one notifies no one; the owner decided these.
- **Order:** a new page goes after the others. Admins move a page with the up and down arrows on it, and each move sends every page's place, so the server refuses a move from a device that missed a page added or deleted meanwhile, and that device loads the pages again. The order isn't encrypted, since it says nothing about what the pages hold.
- **Loading:** the pages' encrypted content, with their files' names, sizes, and keys, comes with everything else a device loads when it opens, as notices do. A page's pictures are fetched once Info is open and they come near the screen, and a document when it's saved.
- **Encryption:** every page is encrypted with the kindergarten's Info Key, made with its first page and kept for good, wrapped with the Staff Key and with the Group Key of every classroom. Every classroom sees every page, so a new key for each page or save, as notices get, would keep no one out, and a classroom added meanwhile could get an outdated copy. An admin's device adding a classroom wraps the key for it in the same request. The database refuses a first page that leaves out a classroom or comes after another, and a classroom added without the key once there is one. A page's content is tied to its page, so the server can't show one page's text in another's place, and its files are sealed as a notice's are. The server knows the pages' order, when each was last saved, which files each carries, and how big they are, not what they say or what the files are called.

## Private inquiries

A family's direct line to its classroom's teachers, kept apart from the board.

- **Reading them:** Messages sits in the app header, whose four destinations are icons only. Each inquiry has an encrypted subject and a conversation of its own, read as a chat: this device's messages on the right, the other side's on the left, under a chip for each day. The inbox lists who each conversation is with, its latest message, and unread state. Filters appear only where they help: subject search above five conversations, open and closed chips once something is closed, and the classroom picker only for families and teachers with more than one classroom. Older messages load in batches of 50.
- **What an admin sets:** Parent messaging, in its own section under a classroom's children — a line saying whether families may write, with the allowance and the week's hours folded into one summary, and the form itself once Change is pressed: an on/off switch, the monthly allowance per family, and one interval per weekday. Messaging starts off, with three inquiries a month; an allowance below one is refused. Saturday and Sunday are closed. Hours and calendar months use Europe/Zagreb, daylight saving included. Holidays aren't detected, and the interface explains that an answer may take longer on non-working days.
- **What an inquiry costs:** a family spends one on every message a teacher hasn't answered yet — the message that starts a conversation, and each further message sent while the family's own is still the last one. Answering a teacher is always free, so an open conversation can't be used to write without limit, and the app says what the next message costs and asks for confirmation before spending one. Teachers are never charged and may send at any time; the switch and hours still apply to every family message. Teachers close inquiries; closed inquiries stay readable and never give an inquiry back. All of a family's devices share the allowance and read state; each teacher has their own. Assigned teachers are notified, and all of a family's devices get replies. Admins reach every classroom, as elsewhere in the app, but are pushed only for the classrooms they teach.
- **Encryption:** subjects, message bodies, and teacher display names use the Family Key. The server keeps only routing, author IDs, ordering, read progress, and policy metadata. A teacher can attach documents and pictures to a message, up to ten of at most 10 MB each, the same kinds a notice carries; each is encrypted with a key of its own inside the message, pictures open on the whole screen and save like a notice's, and documents save as downloads. Families attach nothing, and the server refuses it. A file goes when its inquiry does, and deleting a closed inquiry frees its bytes at once.
- **Lifetime:** an inquiry stays until its family's membership in that classroom is removed, which deletes its messages and read progress. Replacing a QR code doesn't delete conversations. Editing, reopening, and deleting a single message are not supported.

## Individual meetings

- **Offering times:** staff select a date, time window, and meeting length, remove break times, and publish; the home card opens the page for staff and families. Families reserve one time per child per offer, and other families see that a slot is booked without seeing who booked it. Both family cards invited for a child share its reservation. Staff see booked children and can cancel their own meetings; admins manage all offers. Cancelling releases a time, and past offers are removed 90 days after their last slot.
- **Who is invited:** the children and family cards linked when the offer is published. A child or card added later takes part in the next offer. Times use Europe/Zagreb whatever the device's timezone. Names stay encrypted; times and child and family ID links are scheduling metadata. Notifications follow the same generic push.

## Kindergarten access

Where the user experience was concerned, the simplest option won, and the deferred variants need no data migration later.

- **A kindergarten, not a classroom.** An installation holds any number of classrooms and one catalog of children and families. The spec's Teacher Key for each classroom becomes one **Staff Key** for the kindergarten, opened by every staff card. The server enforces which classrooms teachers see and what admins may change; families stay separated by encryption. The [access format](access-format.md) records the trade-off.
- **Roles:** an admin is a teacher who also manages classrooms, teachers, children, and family cards. There can be several, and the last working admin card can't be revoked. Teachers see the children and family cards of their own classrooms and post notices, board photos, and events there. Only admins replace a family's card, because such a card reaches every classroom the family's children are in.
- **First admin:** `npm run deploy` creates the `SETUP_TOKEN` Worker secret when it's missing and prints a setup link (`/app/setup#token=…`) once; `npm run setup-link` replaces the token and prints a new one. Pasting the token works too, and it only permits the first setup. Setup asks for the admin's name only, creates the admin's card and a recovery card, connects the setup device, and ends on a print page that asks for confirmation. Card codes are never stored in the browser: the page retries while open. If the cards weren't printed, the setup device, which stays connected, replaces both.
- **Recovery card:** its printed card and its page explain that it's the spare for when every admin QR code is lost, that only it can then replace them and add children, teachers, and classrooms, and that whoever holds it can do everything an admin can — so it's kept locked away at the kindergarten, apart from everyday QR codes.
- **Classrooms** can be added and renamed, and deleted only when they have no children.
- **Teachers:** Add teacher asks for a name, classrooms, and whether they're an admin, then shows their card. Replacing a lost card keeps the classrooms and the admin setting. If every admin card is lost, the recovery card is the answer.
- **Children and families:** a child has a name and one classroom, and Add child starts from that classroom, so it doesn't ask for one. A family has one named card, suggested as "Horvat family" ("Obitelj Horvat") rather than "Ivana (mum)", which reads better where teachers see which families saw a notice; it is used on every device at home and covers all of the family's children. A sibling is added with the brother's or sister's family cards; parents who live apart get separate cards ("Add another family card"). A family's classrooms follow from its children, so moving a child moves its families' access. Removing a child also removes family cards left without children, after a confirmation that names them. Replace card issues a new card and disconnects every device that used the old one. Add child keeps each new family card on the page and clears the form for the next child, so a classroom's cards print together, and leaving with cards unprinted asks first. A classroom's page replaces any selection of its families' cards at once and prints the new ones together.
- **Cards:** a 128-bit secret as a 28-character typeable code with two check characters, carried in the QR link as `#card=`. Every card has the same format; only the server's records say what a card is. Card derivation is versioned separately from envelopes, with a fixed compatibility test. Cards print plain, on white, two to a row with a dashed edge to cut along. Their QR code fills a round bubble with dots in the brand's dark colours: the code in the middle, with ring-shaped finder patterns and a small round app icon that quartile error correction covers, and random dots around it that keep clear of the finder patterns. A code big enough to have an alignment pattern in its middle leaves the icon out.
- **Connecting:** a phone's camera opens the card link; in the app, Scan QR code reads it with the camera in a dialog, Enter code accepts the typed code, and Choose a photo beside them reads a photo of the card, for devices without a camera or where it isn't allowed. Until a device connects, the header has the language switch instead of Settings. One active card per browser: a working card is never replaced without confirmation, and device keys are stored per card. Parents then see a "You've joined" screen naming their classrooms, which the board replaces.
- **Adding a family's other devices:** a partner's or a grandparent's phone, without the printed QR code that parents may have thrown away. Settings on a family device have Add a device, and Show a QR code makes a one-time card that connects one device within 24 hours — the owner chose a day over 15 minutes or an hour, so a link sent to grandparents far away still works when they open it later that day. The other device scans it, types its code, or opens it as a link sent with Share link. Nobody is signed out and the printed QR code keeps working. A one-time card opens only what the device showing it opens and connects a single device, so once used, or after its day, a photo or a forwarded link of it connects nothing. Because it only adds devices, it can't lock a family out; a teacher's Replace QR code still signs out every device of the family, those added this way included. Whoever holds an unlocked family device could add a device of their own, as they could photograph the printed QR code. A family keeps at most five waiting, and a new one ends the oldest. Only family devices add devices, because a staff card opens the whole kindergarten. Making one takes the unlock key of the device's own card, which family devices keep since this was added, so a device connected before that is asked to sign out and connect again first.
- **Names** of classrooms, teachers, children, and family cards live only in encrypted records.
- **Getting around:** Manage, Info, Messages, and Settings open from the header. Manage holds a tile for each of the viewer's classrooms, every classroom for admins, with Teachers in a section below them; Add classroom turns over to take the new name in its own place. A classroom lists its children, and a child's page shows its family cards, with siblings and their classrooms, and the actions the viewer may take — teachers see only siblings in their own classrooms. Settings show notifications first, then the language, then the device's QR code with Sign out of this device last; on a family device that section also says the kindergarten can give a new QR code if it's lost, because parents who threw the printed one away no longer see that line on it. A page's way back, in its top corner, says Back ("Natrag") and leads a level up: a classroom and the teachers' list to Manage, a teacher to the teachers' list, a child to its classroom, and the other pages home.

## Storage limits

R2 charges once a month's use passes its free allowance, and Cloudflare offers no way to cap that spending. The owner asked that nothing, not even a misused card, can run up a bill. So D1 counts each object BubbleBoard keeps in R2 with its size, and each month's uploads and downloads, before R2 is touched, and refuses more once the limit in `.env` is reached. Teachers are told why an upload was refused, and families why a photo or file didn't open. The counts are for the whole installation and per UTC month, never per family, so they record no one's activity. The Worker and D1 stop at the Free plan's daily limits rather than charging, so R2 is the only resource limited this way. The settings and their defaults are in [hosting](hosting.md#storage).

## Words

- **QR code, not card.** Parents found "card" confusing, so the app and the landing page call what the kindergarten hands out a QR code ("QR kod"): scan your QR code, family QR code, Replace QR code. Code and these docs keep the word card.
- **Notice and notification** in English; Croatian keeps "obavijest" for both, as the landing page does.
- **Odgojiteljica**, in the feminine, throughout the Croatian interface.

## Scope and constraints

- Croatian and English throughout, including install steps and notification text.
- The editor loads only on the page where notices are written. Boards render a notice's stored structure with the app's own components, never as HTML (spec §36). Backgrounds are classes from a fixed set, because the CSP allows no inline styles.
- The server authorizes every request: teachers post, change, and delete in their assigned classrooms, admins everywhere, and families read their children's classrooms. Every staff card opens the same Staff Key, so check each new query against that trade-off ([access format](access-format.md)).
- The Free plan keeps working: queue operations and D1 writes per notice stay small, and nothing polls.
- No offline content, background sync, or notice content in notifications.

## Deferred

- Automatic unread markers and app icon badges.
- Declarative Web Push (iOS 18.4 and later), which needs an encrypted payload.
- Scheduled, pinned, or recurring notices; reactions and comments.
- Marking notices as seen when they're opened rather than with a tap, which would tell the server who read what.
- Showing poll results to families, choosing several answers, and closing a poll.
- A documents area of its own (spec §53–54).
- Expiry for staff cards (spec §32); staff cards are permanent for now.
- A family card replacement that keeps devices connected.
- A kindergarten-wide Families list, and a command that makes a teacher an admin.
- An admin reset for a suspected key compromise; it stays a documented procedure. Revocation stops server access but doesn't erase keys or copies a device already has.
